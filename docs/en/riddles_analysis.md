# Our Discoveries: Deciphering Hidden Codes and Anomalies

This document contains a description of 5 key discoveries made by the authors of this repository during the textual analysis of the five-volume Chinese manuscript.
Unlike classical riddles (known from the tradition of the "I Ching"), these elements are **intentional distortions** or **hidden coordinates** used by the author of the manuscript for encryption.

---

## 1. Omission of the 8th Month in the "Twelve Months" (十二月建) Table (Vol. 3)

*   **Location:** Vol. 3, p. 15.
*   **Original:** Table of correspondence between months and earthly branches.
*   **Anomaly:** After the 7th month (`七月建酉` — Rooster), the 9th month (`九月建戌` — Dog) immediately follows. **The 8th month is completely omitted.**
*   **Our Discovery:** This is not a copyist's error. Vol. 5 (p. 5, hexagram `地澤臨`) contains a direct indication: `至於八月有凶` ("Until the eighth month there will be misfortune"). The omission in Vol. 3 is a **coordinate of catastrophe** pointed to by Vol. 5.
*   **Meaning:** The author encrypted the time of turmoil through a calendar shift.

---

## 2. Indication of the "Ji Day" (己日) in the "Ge" Hexagram (Vol. 5)

*   **Location:** Vol. 5, p. 1.
*   **Original:** `澤火革` (Ge, No. 49 — Revolution). Aphorism: `己日乃孚` ("On the Ji day there will be trust").
*   **Our Discovery:** The "Ji day" is the 6th day of the 60-year cycle. In the context of the "Revolution" hexagram, this is not just a calendar date, but a **starting point** for a change of power.
*   **Meaning:** A direct indication of the day when a coup will occur.

---

## 3. Substitution of a Trigram in "Gou" (Vol. 4)

*   **Location:** Vol. 4, p. 5.
*   **Original:** `天風姤` (Gou, No. 44).
*   **In the text:** `乾上艮下` (Qian above, Gen below).
*   **Canon:** Should be `乾上巽下` (Qian + Xun).
*   **Our Discovery:** Replacing Xun (Wind) with Gen (Mountain) turns "Meeting" (Gou) into "Flight" (Dun, No. 33). This is a conscious distortion of the code.
*   **Meaning:** "A meeting will turn into flight" — a warning of failure.

---

## 4. Substitution of a Trigram in "Tong Ren" (Vol. 4)

*   **Location:** Vol. 4, p. 11.
*   **Original:** `天火同人` (Tong Ren, No. 13).
*   **In the text:** `乾上坎下` (Qian above, Kan below).
*   **Canon:** Should be `乾上離下` (Qian + Li).
*   **Our Discovery:** Replacing Li (Fire) with Kan (Water) turns "Fellow Travelers" (Tong Ren) into "Conflict" (Song, No. 6).
*   **Meaning:** "Instead of unity — conflict" — a classic example of code substitution.

---

## 5. Omission of the Years 壬午 and 癸未 in the 60-Year Cycle Table (Vol. 4)

*   **Location:** Vol. 4, pp. 2-3.
*   **Original:** Table of the 60-year cycle.
*   **In the text:** After `辛巳` (Metal Snake), it immediately goes to `戊子` (Earth Rat).
*   **Our Discovery:** The years `壬午` (Water Horse) and `癸未` (Water Goat) are crossed out.
*   **Meaning:** A negative riddle. In Chinese historiography, crossing out years indicates a period of turmoil or interregnum, when "Heaven did not bestow the mandate."
